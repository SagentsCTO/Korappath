# "The Harvest Carts", a comic for Chapter 2 (draft outline)

**Status:** **Published 17 Nov 2026** at `comics/appuman-harvest/`, with Raju's go-ahead. 15 panels, with the bedtime opening (panels 1–3). The houses follow Raju's descriptions (Kandassankadavu: sandy, palms, canal far off; Sankaramponnath: about ¾ the size of the tharavadu, broad gate, plastered wall, no barn). See `houses` in `panels.json`.
- Raju chose to keep the helpers staying with the bullocks and stacking the sacks (panels 8, 10, 11), although the chapter says the cart drivers went home that night.
- The end card's "asleep at the desk next morning" scene was not drawn; the end card just says "Next time: Kunjan's rickshaw!"

Source: `stories/appuman-harvest.html`, *Appuman, a warm-hearted farmer*.

It picks up where Chapter 1's comic ended ("Next time: who was Appuman?"). At bedtime, Achachan tells the kids about Appuman: the things Achan told us that aren't in the chapter. After he falls asleep, they sneak into his study, open the book, and the time travel begins. Appu (10) and Achu (4) land at dawn in Kandassankadavu, where Appuman is setting off with the harvest carts. They ride the carts the long way to Sankaramponnath in Chiyyaram, arriving in the evening with him. There they meet young Achan, who wears the new half-trousers, and stay for his visit.

**15 panels.** Panel 5 (dawn at Kandassankadavu) was added on 16 Nov 2026, and panels 1–3 (bedtime story and the sneak) on 17 Nov 2026, to tell history from Achan that isn't in the chapter.

## Rules (same as Chapter 1)

- **Captions** (the ivory boxes) are quoted word for word, in Malayalam and in the existing English translation.
  - "…" marks words left out. Nothing is reworded.
- **Speech bubbles** are invented children's dialogue.
  - Appuman, a real person, only says what the chapter says he said.
  - Young Achan's lines are kept minimal.
- **Achan's extra history is told by Achachan himself, as a bedtime story** (Raju's idea, 17 Nov 2026), in panels 1–2.
  - His lines are our wording of what Achan told us. Every fact comes from his messages; nothing is added.
  - Later panels have the kids recognise those details ("just like Achachan said"), so the history appears in words as well as in the pictures.
  - This replaces the separate "From Achan's memories" note boxes, which were drafted earlier the same day.
- **Pictures contain no text.**

## History from Achan to show in the pictures (not in the chapter)
From Achan's messages of 16 Nov 2026. These details are shown **in the pictures only**. Captions stay word for word from the chapter.

- **Appuman** looked after Grandpa's vast paddy fields. He studied up to 8th standard. He was about 6 ft tall and well built.
- **Clothes:**
  - Cart trips: a clean mundu and a sleeveless shirt.
  - At home: a long mundu only, with a head cover.
- **Where he lived:** permanently at Kandassankadavu, with Grandpa and Grandpa's sisters. One of the sisters was **Devi chechi**, Appuman's mother.
- **Achamma** was Grandpa's beloved wife. She lived at Sankaramponnath in Chiyyaram.
- **The journey:** about 25 km by two or three bullock carts, at the end of each season.
  - The chapter puts Grandpa's home at "about ten miles west of Thrissur". These are Achan's own figures, recorded as given.
- **Shown in:**
  - panels 1–2: told by Achachan at bedtime
  - panel 5 (Kandassankadavu, Grandpa, Devi chechi, his cart-trip clothes)
  - panel 6 (the long road)
  - panels 7–11 (cart-trip clothes)
  - panels 12 and 14 (home clothes); panel 13 (the oracle costume)
  - his height and build everywhere

## Panels

### 1. Bedtime story (4:5), NEW
Present day, night. Achachan lies propped on pillows in bed, in a plain white sleeveless vest (from Raju, 17 Nov 2026) with his glasses on, under a soft bedside lamp. Appu and Achu are tucked in on either side of him, wide awake and listening.

- **Achachan:** "Appuman lived at Kandassankadavu, by the canal, with my grandfather and his sisters. His mother was Devi chechi. He looked after all my grandfather's paddy fields."
- **Achachan:** "At the end of every season he brought the harvest to us at Sankaramponnath, where my Achamma lived. That was two or three bullock carts, about 25 km!"
- **Appu:** "Was he big and strong?"
- **Achachan:** "Tall, about six feet!"

### 2. The oracle, told in bed (4:5), NEW
Achachan, still lying down, lifts both arms with his eyes wide, acting it out. Achu squeals and pulls the blanket up to her nose. Appu is laughing.

### 13. The oracle act (4:5)
*From Achan's description, 16 Nov 2026.* Appuman mimicked the local komaram in front of the children to amuse them. His sword was a **sharpened coconut-frond stalk (പട്ട)**. He would begin with a short, still meditation, then his whole body would start trembling, slowly and gradually. All of a sudden would come a roar and an outburst of anger, as if Durga Bhagavathy had entered him. Then he'd command each child in "divine language", like the Goddess speaking to Her subjects.

- **The picture:** the instant the stillness breaks.
  - Appuman's eyes are blazing, mid-roar. He holds the sharpened coconut-frond stalk high like a sword and points it at one child as if giving a command.
  - Faint tremble lines show the shaking.
  - The children (cousins and brothers, plus Appu and Achu) are frozen between awe and giggles. Achu peeks through her fingers.
  - **Costume, from Achan:**
    - **No head cover.** His white cloth is worn as a **sash**: over one shoulder, diagonally across his bare chest to the opposite hip, with the end hanging down. Achan demonstrated this on a video call, 16 Nov 2026.
    - His mundu is worn like a **പാളസ്സാർ**, the Brahmin priests' temple style. **Confirmed by Achan as തറ്റുടുക്കൽ (thattudukkal)**: the cloth goes round the waist, a portion passes between the thighs, it is tucked in front and behind, and the front is pleated.
      - Source: Dhanya B Nair, "Impact of Socio Economic Transition on the Traditional Dress Culture of Middle Kerala", IJFMR 5(2), 2023.
  - **His hair is short and swept back**, with nothing on the forehead, and he runs his fingers through it as a komaram does.
- **Leave out:** any image of the goddess, red silk, anklets, a real sword, and anything mocking. It's an affectionate family memory of a playful imitation; red silk and anklets aren't mentioned by Achan.
- **Caption (ml):** അപ്പുമാൻ മുറ്റിച്ചൂർ അമ്പലത്തിലെ കോമരത്തിനെ അനുകരിച്ചു തുള്ളലും അട്ടഹാസങ്ങളും ഒക്കെ ചെയ്യും. ബഹു തമാശക്കാരനാണ് ഞങ്ങളുടെ അപ്പുമാൻ.
- **Caption (en):** Appuman would imitate the temple oracle of Muttichoor, leaping about and shouting. Our Appuman was a great joker.
- **Appu:** "The oracle! Just like Achachan told us!"
- **Achu:** "Again! Again!"
- **Appuman:** no bubble. His "divine language" stays in the picture, so no words are invented for him.

### 15. Goodbye, and home (4:5)
Morning, two days later. Appuman sets off down the lane. Young Achan and the village children wave sadly. The golden swirl rises around Appu and Achu as they wave too.
- **Caption (ml):** രണ്ടു ദിവസം കഴിഞ്ഞു അദ്ദേഹം തിരിച്ചുപോകുമ്പോൾ ഞങ്ങൾ കുട്ടികൾ വിഷമത്തോടെയും വിഷാദത്തോടെയും ആകും യാത്ര അയക്കുക.

- **End card:** next morning, Achachan finds the two of them asleep at his desk with the book still open. They wake up and do Appuman's planting-song gestures for him, and he laughs. "Next time: Kunjan's rickshaw!"
